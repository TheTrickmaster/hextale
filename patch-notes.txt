## v0.79.30
- Little Mermaid gains her +2 to every side again. Her card says "Gains +2 ALL when played, but discards a card from your hand", and only the discard was happening. The two halves of that sentence are handled by two different parts of the game, and when one of them was not something the engine could work out on its own, both were dropped — so the half it could do perfectly well went with it.

## v0.79.29
- The AI now brings a deck that costs what yours costs. Deck capacity is the sum of the rarity costs — timeless 4, mythic 3, rare 2, common 1 — and the game gives you 24 points to spend. The AI was spending all 24 every single game, whoever it was playing: against a starter deck it came to the table with twelve rares. It now spends exactly what you spent.
- The old balancing did exist, but it matched the wrong thing: card power, which is the sum of the numbers on the sides. Power cannot see abilities, and abilities are most of what separates a common from a rare — so two decks could balance on paper and not be the same game at all.

## v0.79.28
- The damage bubbles are gone. The drawings they were dressed in had been deleted, so every match was asking the site for four files that no longer exist; the bubbles themselves had stopped flying back when scoring replaced damage, and only the machinery was left. Nothing about the score changed: the difference between attacker and defender, and one point for each of your own cards on the board at the end of a turn, are counted exactly as before.
- The specular sheen is back on cards above level 1. In the full-screen card view the arrows redraw the card at the level you pick, but the sheen was still asking the card its real level — so stepping up to 2, 3 or 4 showed the foil and the parallax and never the sheen.
- The red dot on "Library & decks" now sits at the right end of the button instead of next to the words, which it used to push off-centre.
- And it goes away once you have seen the new cards. It cleared only for cards the mouse happened to pass over, one at a time, and a collection is read by scrolling — so it stayed on forever. Now a card counts as seen when it has actually been on your screen.
- The two lines inside "Card packs" sit 2 pixels apart. They were written to be 0 and were drawn at 10: a stronger rule further down the stylesheet was quietly overriding it.

## v0.79.27
- The "a newer version is available" notice no longer offers you a file that is not the game. It was pointing at the site's new 404 page: for the check, any .html in the top folder counted as a version, and until that page appeared there were none at all up there — so it had been quiet not because it agreed, but because it was looking at an empty shelf. A file now counts as a version only if its name carries a version number, and the game will not point you at anything that is not newer than what you are running.

## v0.79.26
- Press Collect and the letter rises out of the way and fades, taking the dark behind it with it.
- No button is narrower than 200 pixels any more. A button is five pieces of art in a row — two caps, a centre, and two fillers that take up what is left — and below about 156 pixels the fillers have nothing left to fill and the three images end up stacked on each other. That is what 'small and crooked' was: a button shorter than its own frame.
- The level row in the full card view sits at the bottom of the panel, where it belongs. A 16-pixel margin was quietly cancelling the one that pushes it down there.
- The hexagon helper starts off on a new account. It was on, which meant starting your first game with a hand-hold you never asked for; it is one tick in the settings for anyone who wants it.

## v0.79.25
- The Collect button on the new-deck screen is the solid one now, not the see-through one.

## v0.79.24
- The last beat of Pick a letter reads better: a dark veil falls over the deck art from the top down, "New deck unlocked!" sits above the deck, the line below counts the cards that just reached your library, and the button says "Collect".

## v0.79.23
- The starter deck that was called "Starter Debuff" is now "Starter Trickster".
- Pick a letter, polished: the title reads like every other window's, the Pick buttons run the full width of their column with the label in the middle, and hovering a letter brightens it.
- And picking one is now a moment. The card-draw sound as the letter is taken, the other two fold away, the frame around them and the title go with them — only your letter is left — and when it lands in the middle it flashes, dings, and opens: the deck drops in from above, the message rises from below, and the column takes on the art of the deck you just got.

## v0.79.22
- New: you pick your first deck. Right after choosing your name, three letters are laid out — one from a friend, one from a lover, one from a stranger — and the deck you get is the one whose letter you open. Until today the game drew it for you, without saying so: the first thing it did on your behalf was make a choice in your place.
- The letters do not say aggressive, supportive or risky. They are letters, and the tone is the clue.
- Pick one and the other two fold away, the one you chose slides to the middle and lights up, and the letter gives way to your new deck.
- You choose once. The deck is yours from then on, and it is yours to change in the Library like any other.
- Strigoi no longer asks for a laugh that was never recorded. That sound belonged to the hand-written version of the ability; now Strigoi turns into Dark Strigoi through the sheet, and a card that transforms speaks with the new card's voice — which does exist.

## v0.79.21
- One fewer failed request on every load: a placeholder from before the cards had real art was still named in the source, and the preloader dutifully went looking for a file that has not existed for a long time.
