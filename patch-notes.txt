## v0.79.42
- Keeping a second card costs the price of the CHEAPER of the two, whichever order you pick them in. For two versions it was the price of the one you picked second, which turned the order of your clicks into a lever: the same pair cost 50 or 200 depending on which you touched first. The button now shows what you will actually pay, and the server charges the same.
- The glow and the lit border behind an unpacked card lean with the card. They have their own layer that was reading the tilt from a different branch of the page, so they always saw zero and stood still.
- Turning a card throws sparks out from behind it, along a winding path rather than a straight line, fast at first and slowing as they fade. How many, what colour and how long they last is the card rarity: a handful of white for a common, a cloud of orange for a timeless.
- Dropping an envelope in the middle now travels there, fast then slowing, instead of drifting in on a spring. A spring is the right motion for chasing a moving pointer; aimed at a fixed target it reads as hesitation.
- The label above a card is frosted glass like every other panel in the game.
- The burst of light is another fifth larger.
- The new-cards dot could get stuck on forever. A card counts as new until you look at it, and you can only look at it if it is in your collection — so a card left in your account whose name the sheet no longer knows was new for good, with no gesture able to clear it. Cards that cannot be looked at no longer count. There is also a debug button that marks everything as seen.

## v0.79.39
- The envelope tilts properly while you drag it. It was not tilting at all: the rotation had no perspective behind it, so left and right produced the same flat squash. It now leans the way you are moving it, like a card.
- Drag it back down over the pack rows and it dims: that is where letting go puts it away. It used to jump to the middle of the screen for an instant before vanishing, which read as a bug because it was one.
- Every tap on the wax is a different jolt, with a different one of four impact sounds. The shake used to be one fixed animation replayed five times, and by the third you could see it coming.
- The chips of wax have gravity now. The tap throws them up and sideways, then they fall, and they keep falling until they are off the screen instead of fading out in mid-air. They come in more sizes and two families of shape.
- The hint under the seal is frosted glass with a sheen running across it every two seconds, and the arrow above it pulses upward. It steps out of the way at the first tap and comes back if you stop for three seconds.
- The seal lights up when you point at it. It is the only part of the envelope you can click, and it should say so.
- The discarded card fades out instead of burning. The fire was beautiful and said the wrong thing — that the card you did not keep gets destroyed, rather than simply not taken. Two hundred and forty lines of ash went with it.
- Above each card there is one label now, in a window-shaped box: Owned, or New! on red. It used to be a painted ribbon in one case and a bare line of text in the other — two languages for the same question.
- The buttons under the cards are 300 wide and no longer jump right when pressed. The press style replaced their whole transform, centring included, so for one frame they lost the offset that holds them under their card.
- Opening a pack no longer stalls if the answer from the server is late. The cards are asked for the moment you drop the envelope and normally arrive long before the seal breaks, but on a slow line they did not, and the envelope opened onto nothing.
- The debug menu can hand you 100 magic ink, a Treasure pack or a Reward pack. They ask the server, because that is where ink and packs actually live.

## v0.79.38
- Packs are envelopes now, and you open them with your hands. Drag one to the middle of the screen — it tilts and catches the light as it moves, the way a card does — let go, and it settles in the centre.
- Then you break the seal. Five taps on the wax: it cracks a little more each time, the envelope jolts, and chips of sealing wax fly out from behind it. On the fifth the seal shatters into three pieces, the envelope starts shaking, the flap snaps open and the light bursts out.
- Three cards come out of it, not two. They rise from between the paper of the envelope, shoot off the top of the screen and come back down to the middle, exactly as they did before.
- You keep one for free. The second costs magic ink — the price of that card's own rarity, so which one you pick second is the one you pay for — and the third is always discarded.
- The book that used to sit in the middle and tear itself open is gone, and so are the two hundred and fifty lines that drew it.

## v0.79.36
- "Card packs" is a different page. Packs are things you own now: a timed one on the left, everything else on the right, in a row that scrolls once you have more than four. You pick the one you want to open instead of opening "the pack".
- There are three kinds. A daily pack matures on its own every 12 hours (it was 8) and does not stack — the clock restarts when you open it, not when it ripens, so leaving it there costs you nothing. A reward pack is the one you win by playing. A treasure pack is the one you buy, 100 magic ink, with the yellow button on the right.
- The Dark/Light switch moved up into the title bar, and says which version you are looking at instead of which one you would get. It used to appear for a moment between the two cards and say the opposite thing depending on when you read it.
- Opening, the cards, and Collect are exactly what they were. The page around them changed; the sequence did not.

## v0.79.35
- Card art now loads whatever format you saved it in: .jpg, .png or .jpeg. The game only ever looked for .jpg, and it did so silently — it asked the site for an address that does not exist, found nothing, and left the placeholder. Five characters had their art sitting on the site in .png and had never once been seen.
- The three formats cost nothing at startup. Which illustrations exist is worked out once, at import, and written into the catalogue; what you download is the answer. Only cards still without art get checked again while you play, and those are exactly the ones the two extra formats are for.
